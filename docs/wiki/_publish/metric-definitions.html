Metric Definitions

How every number in the sweep comparison table and the window view is computed: which gem5 statistics it reads, the formula, what it leaves out, and where it can mislead.

Conventions

Statistic names are shortened with these prefixes.

WrittenStands for
core.board.processor.start.core.
l1i., l1d., l2.board.cache_hierarchy.l1icaches., .l1dcaches., .l2caches.
::inst, ::datathe L2's per-requester value, ::processor.start.core.inst and ::processor.start.core.data: requests that came from the L1I and from the L1D

Stats blocks and the window value

A run dumps and resets its statistics every --stats-interval-insts instructions (200K in the sweeps), so stats.txt holds one block per interval. A metric's window value is its formula applied to the statistics summed over all blocks of the detailed window. It is never the mean of the block values: window IPC is total instructions over total cycles.

Instructions (insts)

From intervals.csv: gem5's committed-instruction count, which on the O3 core leaves out NOPs and instruction prefetches. core.commitStats0.numInsts includes NOPs (about 2% in one checked window), so the two are not mixed in one ratio.

Cycles

core.numCycles of the block. It leaves out the time the guest sits in WFI (those cycles go to quiesceCycles). IPC and every per-cycle share describe the core while it runs, not wall time.

A statistic gem5 did not print

gem5 leaves a statistic out of a block while it is zero. A missing statistic reads as 0. A metric none of whose statistics ever appears is not shown; one that is zero in every block is not shown either, except the top-down and cache-hierarchy rows.

PKI

Events per 1000 committed instructions: the summed count × 1000 / insts.

% cycles

The summed count / core.numCycles. For the back-end rows the count is an event the pipeline bumps once per stalled cycle.

Wrong-path work

Unless a row says it is counted at commit, it includes instructions and accesses that were later squashed. Every cache statistic does.

The comparison table

Per suite, column A is the baseline and the other columns are changes against it. For rates and counts: the geometric mean of A's window values, and the geometric mean of each checkpoint's ratio to A, shown as a change (ratio − 1). A checkpoint where either value is zero has no ratio and is left out ("excl. n"); a mean over fewer than half the checkpoints is not shown. For the top-down and cache-hierarchy rows, whose parts add up: the plain mean over checkpoints and the difference from A, in percentage points or cycles. Plain means are not weighted by a checkpoint's accesses or instructions.

Slice categories and edge blocks (agentic)

A category's value uses only the slices of that category. Its cycles are the slices' simulated time over the clock period, so they include WFI. Its event counts are shared out from the stats blocks by instruction overlap, so a short slice inherits part of its neighbours' events. With edge blocks dropped, every metric is recomputed over the remaining blocks.

Cache hierarchy

Two sides: instruction (demand accesses of the L1I: fetch requests for one line, not instructions) and data (demand accesses of the L1D: loads and stores). Page-table walks go through the MMU cache and are on neither side.

gem5's classic cache counts a demand access as a hit or a miss. A miss either allocates a miss-status holding register (MSHR) and sends one request to the level below (an MSHR miss, here a new miss), or finds an MSHR already outstanding for its line and waits on it (an MSHR hit, here in flight). The outstanding MSHR can belong to an earlier demand miss or to a prefetch.

Ownership (data)

amat_own_d
Formula
N1·o2/R × (TW/P + W·r1 − W·ℓ1) / W / A
What
The ownership requests, each at the mean time of a whole-line write miss of the L1D.
Notes
Measured for single whole-line stores (W); the same mean is assumed for stores that only together cover a line and for upgrades (in gin-2121 without prefetchers W is 1.63M of 2.11M ownership requests). Zero when the window has no whole-line store.

L2

amat_l2_i amat_l2_d
Formula
(Tnew − N1·ℓ1) / A − memory − L2 in flight − ownership
What
The rest of the new misses' time: the way from the L1 to the L2 and back and the L2's lookup, paid by every new miss whether the L2 has the line or not.
Notes
A remainder, so it also holds what the other parts miss. In a single stats block a request that straddles the block edge can make the other parts exceed the L1's time; they are then scaled to fit and this part is 0.

How exact

  • Hits, in-flight and new misses are exact. M1 = J1 + N1 holds with no remainder (gin-2121, no prefetch, data: 44,960,829 = 28,167,170 + 16,793,659).
  • New L1 misses against L2 requests. Instruction side: 13,192,502 new L1I misses, 13,192,327 L2 requests. Data side: 16,793,659 new L1D misses − 2,078,056 invalidations − 30,999 upgrades + 83,325 atomics, load-exclusives and software prefetches (not demand at the L1D, demand reads at the L2) = 14,767,929, against 14,759,278 L2 demand requests: 0.06% apart.
  • The time split. Fitted per stats block, the L1's new-miss time equals the L2's own miss latency plus a fixed cost per request: 14.9 cycles per L2 hit and 2.2 on top of the L2's miss latency per L2 miss (instruction), 18.2 and 5.1 (loads); the total residual is 0.02% and 0.21% (gin-2121, no prefetch). So Memory is what the L2 measured, and the few cycles of path per L2 miss are in L2.
  • Source of all three: runs/no-prefetch/reference/gin-2121-nopf.cache-stats.txt, summed over runs/sweep/20261113-1805-fe8-l1i1-nopf/agentic/gin-2121.

What AMAT leaves out

  • Time before the cache accepts an access. A miss latency starts when the cache takes the request. While the L1D is blocked the core retries, and that wait is in no cache statistic. The Blocked rows show when this matters: the L1D is blocked for 5.9% of cycles on average in the agentic suite with prefetchers and 12.8% without; in 736.ocio_r, 40% and 82% (runs/sweep/20261113-1805-compare-nopf/compare.view.json). Data AMAT is understated there.
  • Wrong-path accesses are counted like any other. In gin-2121 without prefetchers the L1D saw 242.4M load accesses and 224.5M loads committed, some of which were forwarded from a store and never reached the cache.
  • Forwarded loads are absent. They make no cache access, so "per access" is not "per load".
  • Atomics, load-exclusives and software prefetches are not demand accesses at the L1D and are not in A (1.1M atomic hits against 365.9M demand hits in gin-2121).
  • AMAT is not pipeline cost. Misses overlap. A store accesses the cache after it commits, so its wait holds a store-queue entry, not the pipeline; loads and stores are in one data bar.
  • Translation time (TLB misses, page walks) is before the access and not in it.
  • With a perfect L1 the side has no accesses and shows N/A. With a perfect L2 the parts of the new misses show N/A; the L1 shares and the AMAT total remain.

Reading rules.
  • Runs made before the 2026-11-13 fix report front-end bound too high and back-end bound too low; do not compare shares across that date.
  • Memory bound understates what a perfect L1D saves (it predicted +14% for agentic and +17% for SPEC against +43% and +47% measured).
  • A split with no events puts its parent's share in its last part.
