# Metric definitions

How every number in the sweep comparison table and the window view is computed: which gem5 statistics it reads, the formula, what it leaves out, and where it can mislead. Generated by `docs/tools/metric_definitions.py`, which also writes the published page ([[publish]], `metric-definitions`): edit the content there, not here. The code is `window_view.py` (`METRICS`, `topdown`, `cache_values`) and `sweep_summary.py`; the pages that show the metrics are in [[viewers]] and [[sweeps]].

## Conventions

Prefixes in the stat columns:

- `core.`: `board.processor.start.core.`
- `l1i.`, `l1d.`, `l2.`: `board.cache_hierarchy.l1icaches.`, `.l1dcaches.`, `.l2caches.`
- `::inst`, `::data`: the L2's per-requester value, `::processor.start.core.inst` and `::processor.start.core.data`: requests that came from the L1I and from the L1D

- **Stats blocks and the window value.** A run dumps and resets its statistics every `--stats-interval-insts` instructions (200K in the sweeps), so `stats.txt` holds one block per interval. A metric's window value is its formula applied to the statistics summed over all blocks of the detailed window. It is never the mean of the block values: window IPC is total instructions over total cycles.
- **Instructions (`insts`).** From `intervals.csv`: gem5's committed-instruction count, which on the O3 core leaves out NOPs and instruction prefetches. `core.commitStats0.numInsts` includes NOPs (about 2% in one checked window), so the two are not mixed in one ratio.
- **Cycles.** `core.numCycles` of the block. It leaves out the time the guest sits in WFI (those cycles go to `quiesceCycles`). IPC and every per-cycle share describe the core while it runs, not wall time.
- **A statistic gem5 did not print.** gem5 leaves a statistic out of a block while it is zero. A missing statistic reads as 0. A metric none of whose statistics ever appears is not shown; one that is zero in every block is not shown either, except the top-down and cache-hierarchy rows.
- **PKI.** Events per 1000 committed instructions: the summed count × 1000 / `insts`.
- **% cycles.** The summed count / `core.numCycles`. For the back-end rows the count is an event the pipeline bumps once per stalled cycle.
- **Wrong-path work.** Unless a row says it is counted at commit, it includes instructions and accesses that were later squashed. Every cache statistic does.
- **The comparison table.** Per suite, column A is the baseline and the other columns are changes against it. For rates and counts: the geometric mean of A's window values, and the geometric mean of each checkpoint's ratio to A, shown as a change (ratio − 1). A checkpoint where either value is zero has no ratio and is left out ("excl. n"); a mean over fewer than half the checkpoints is not shown. For the top-down and cache-hierarchy rows, whose parts add up: the plain mean over checkpoints and the difference from A, in percentage points or cycles. Plain means are not weighted by a checkpoint's accesses or instructions.
- **Slice categories and edge blocks (agentic).** A category's value uses only the slices of that category. Its cycles are the slices' simulated time over the clock period, so they include WFI. Its event counts are shared out from the stats blocks by instruction overlap, so a short slice inherits part of its neighbours' events. With edge blocks dropped, every metric is recomputed over the remaining blocks.

Related: [[aggregating-metrics]], [[instruction-counts]], [[numcycles-excludes-wfi]], [[o3-wrong-path-counting]], [[comparing-configurations]].

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1D misses | `l1d_mpki` | PKI | `l1d.overallMisses::total` | misses × 1000 / `insts` | Every access that did not find its line: new misses, accesses joining a miss in flight, ownership requests, software prefetches. Wrong-path loads are included. The *Cache hierarchy* rows separate these. |

| Completed page walks (all, data, instruction) | `walks_done_pki`, `walks_done_d_pki`, `walks_done_i_pki` | PKI | `core.mmu.walker.requestOrigin_Completed::Data`, `::Inst` | completed × 1000 / `insts` | The walk count to use. |
| L1D miss latency, L1I miss latency | `l1d_lat`, `l1i_lat` | cycles | `l1d.overallMissLatency::total`, `l1d.overallMisses::total` (`l1i.` likewise); the block's cycles and ticks | (`overallMissLatency` / `overallMisses`) × `numCycles` / ticks | Mean over all misses, joined ones included. The AMAT rows convert with the clock period and do not have this error. |

## Cache hierarchy

Two sides: **instruction** (demand accesses of the L1I: fetch requests for one line, not instructions) and **data** (demand accesses of the L1D: loads and stores). Page-table walks go through the MMU cache and are on neither side.

gem5's classic cache counts a *demand* access as a hit or a miss. A miss either allocates a miss-status holding register (MSHR) and sends one request to the level below (an **MSHR miss**, here a *new miss*), or finds an MSHR already outstanding for its line and waits on it (an **MSHR hit**, here *in flight*). The outstanding MSHR can belong to an earlier demand miss or to a prefetch.

Symbols (statistics summed over the window; `1` is the side's L1, `2` the L2):

| symbol | meaning |
|---|---|
| `H1`, `M1` | `demandHits::total`, `demandMisses::total` of the L1I or L1D |
| `J1`, `N1` | `demandMshrHits::total` (in flight), `demandMshrMisses::total` (new misses); `M1` = `J1` + `N1` |
| `T1` | `demandMissLatency::total`: per missing access, its arrival at the L1 to its response, summed (ticks) |
| `U1` | `demandMshrMissLatency::total`: per new miss, its arrival to the fill, before the L1's response latency (ticks) |
| `h2`, `m2`, `j2`, `n2`, `t2`, `u2` | the same six statistics of the L2 for the side's requester (`l2.demandHits::inst` or `::data`, and so on) |
| `o2` | ownership requests the L2 got from the L1D: `l2.InvalidateReq`, `l2.UpgradeReq` and `l2.SCUpgradeReq`, `.hits::data` + `.misses::data` |
| `W`, `TW` | `l1d.WriteLineReq.mshrMisses::total` and `l1d.WriteLineReq.mshrMissLatency::total`: new L1D misses of stores that write a whole line |
| `P` | clock period in ticks: `clock` of `[board.clk_domain]` in `config.ini` (333) |
| `ℓ1`, `ℓ2` | hit latency in cycles, from `config.ini`: the larger of `tag_latency` and `data_latency` (their sum with `sequential_access`). L1I 1 in these sweeps, L1D 4, L2 11 |
| `r1`, `r2` | `response_latency` of the L1 and the L2 (cycles) |
| `A` | `H1` + `M1`: the side's demand accesses |
| `R` | `h2` + `m2` + `o2`: what the L1's new misses became at the L2 |

| metric | key | formula | what it is | leaves out, pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Served by the L1 | `res_l1_i`, `res_l1_d` | `H1` / `A` | The L1's demand hit rate. Measured. | A line a prefetcher brought in counts as a hit, so with prefetchers on the hit rate includes their effect. |
| In flight to the L1 | `res_l1fly_i`, `res_l1fly_d` | `J1` / `A` | Accesses that missed on a line the L1 was already fetching and waited for that fill. They make no request to the L2. Measured. | Where the awaited line comes from (L2 or memory) is not known per access. With prefetchers on these are mostly accesses that arrive while a prefetch is still under way; the line then appears in no demand L2 or memory count. |
| Served by the L2 | `res_l2_i`, `res_l2_d` | `N1` / `A` × `h2` / `R` | New L1 misses that hit in the L2. | The L1's new misses and the L2's requests are the same requests, counted on both sides, so the share is exact up to their difference (see *How exact*). |
| In flight to the L2 | `res_l2fly_i`, `res_l2fly_d` | `N1` / `A` × `j2` / `R` | New L1 misses that joined a miss or prefetch the L2 already had outstanding. | The fill they wait for always comes from memory, so for provenance read this together with *Served by memory*. Without prefetchers it is nearly zero, because each L1 already merges its requests per line. With the L2 prefetcher on it is mostly requests landing on a prefetch. |
| Served by memory | `res_mem_i`, `res_mem_d` | `N1` / `A` × `n2` / `R` | New L1 misses that were also new L2 misses. | None beyond the above. |
| Ownership only (data) | `res_own_d` | `N1` / `A` × `o2` / `R` | Stores whose L1D miss moved no data. Either the store, or the stores waiting on one MSHR together, cover a whole line: the L1D asks only for ownership (`InvalidateReq`; `DC ZVA`, as memset and the kernel's page clearing use, is the common source). Or the store hits a line the L1D holds read-only (`UpgradeReq`). | At the L1D both are counted as ordinary demand misses. At the L2 neither is a demand request. An invalidation that misses in the L2 is answered by the memory bus, not by DRAM. |
| L2 hit rate | `l2_hit_i`, `l2_hit_d` | `h2` / (`h2` + `m2`) | Of the L2's demand requests from that side. | Ownership requests are not in it. Requests from the L1 prefetchers are under their own requester and not in it either. |
| AMAT | `amat_i`, `amat_d` | (`A`·`ℓ1` + `T1`/`P` − `M1`·`ℓ1`) / `A` | Average time of a demand access as the L1 sees it: the hit latency for every access, plus each missing access's measured wait past it. | See *What AMAT leaves out*. |
| L1 lookup | `amat_l1_i`, `amat_l1_d` | `ℓ1` | The configured hit latency. | Assumed, not measured: gem5 prints no hit latency in these runs. |
| L1 in flight | `amat_l1fly_i`, `amat_l1fly_d` | (`T1`/`P` − `Tnew` − `J1`·`ℓ1`) / `A`, with `Tnew` = `U1`/`P` + `N1`·`r1` | The waits of the accesses that joined an outstanding L1 miss or prefetch. `Tnew` is the time of the new misses: gem5 adds the response latency to the first access's miss latency but not to the MSHR's. | Measured, as a difference of two measured sums. |
| Memory | `amat_mem_i`, `amat_mem_d` | (`u2`/`P` + `n2`·`r2` − `n2`·`ℓ2`) / `A` | The time the L2's new misses for this side took past the L2's lookup: what the L2 itself waited on memory. | Measured at the L2. |
| L2 in flight | `amat_l2fly_i`, `amat_l2fly_d` | (`t2`/`P` − `u2`/`P` − `n2`·`r2` − `j2`·`ℓ2`) / `A` | The time requests of this side waited in the L2 on a miss or prefetch it already had outstanding. | Measured at the L2. |
| Ownership (data) | `amat_own_d` | `N1`·`o2`/`R` × (`TW`/`P` + `W`·`r1` − `W`·`ℓ1`) / `W` / `A` | The ownership requests, each at the mean time of a whole-line write miss of the L1D. | Measured for single whole-line stores (`W`); the same mean is assumed for stores that only together cover a line and for upgrades (in gin-2121 without prefetchers `W` is 1.63M of 2.11M ownership requests). Zero when the window has no whole-line store. |
| L2 | `amat_l2_i`, `amat_l2_d` | (`Tnew` − `N1`·`ℓ1`) / `A` − memory − L2 in flight − ownership | The rest of the new misses' time: the way from the L1 to the L2 and back and the L2's lookup, paid by every new miss whether the L2 has the line or not. | A remainder, so it also holds what the other parts miss. In a single stats block a request that straddles the block edge can make the other parts exceed the L1's time; they are then scaled to fit and this part is 0. |
| Blocked (L1I, L1D, L2) | `blk_l1i`, `blk_l1d`, `blk_l2` | Σ `blockedCycles::no_mshrs`, `::no_targets`, `::no_wbuffers` / `core.numCycles` | Share of cycles the cache accepted no new request. An access that arrives then is refused and retried by the core. | Cache clock cycles over core cycles; they differ only in windows with WFI. The blocked time is a property of the cache, not a wait per access: it does not say how many accesses were waiting. |
| Blocked by cause | `blk_*_mshr`, `blk_*_tgt`, `blk_*_wb` | `blockedCycles::no_mshrs` (no free MSHR), `::no_targets` (an MSHR's target list is full: 8 accesses per MSHR in the L1D), `::no_wbuffers` (no free write buffer), each / `core.numCycles` | The parts of the row above; they add up to it. | gem5 credits a whole blocked period to the cause that clears last, so a period that began for one cause and ended for another is counted under the second. |

The served-from rows add up to 100% and the AMAT parts to AMAT.

### How exact

- **Hits, in-flight and new misses are exact.** `M1` = `J1` + `N1` holds with no remainder (gin-2121, no prefetch, data: 44,960,829 = 28,167,170 + 16,793,659).
- **New L1 misses against L2 requests.** Instruction side: 13,192,502 new L1I misses, 13,192,327 L2 requests. Data side: 16,793,659 new L1D misses − 2,078,056 invalidations − 30,999 upgrades + 83,325 atomics, load-exclusives and software prefetches (not demand at the L1D, demand reads at the L2) = 14,767,929, against 14,759,278 L2 demand requests: 0.06% apart.
- **The time split.** Fitted per stats block, the L1's new-miss time equals the L2's own miss latency plus a fixed cost per request: 14.9 cycles per L2 hit and 2.2 on top of the L2's miss latency per L2 miss (instruction), 18.2 and 5.1 (loads); the total residual is 0.02% and 0.21% (gin-2121, no prefetch). So *Memory* is what the L2 measured, and the few cycles of path per L2 miss are in *L2*.
- Source of all three: `runs/no-prefetch/reference/gin-2121-nopf.cache-stats.txt`, summed over `runs/sweep/20261113-1805-fe8-l1i1-nopf/agentic/gin-2121`.

### What AMAT leaves out

- **Time before the cache accepts an access.** A miss latency starts when the cache takes the request. While the L1D is blocked the core retries, and that wait is in no cache statistic. The *Blocked* rows show when this matters: the L1D is blocked for 5.9% of cycles on average in the agentic suite with prefetchers and 12.8% without; in 736.ocio_r, 40% and 82% (`runs/sweep/20261113-1805-compare-nopf/compare.view.json`). Data AMAT is understated there.
- **Wrong-path accesses are counted** like any other. In gin-2121 without prefetchers the L1D saw 242.4M load accesses and 224.5M loads committed, some of which were forwarded from a store and never reached the cache.
- **Forwarded loads are absent.** They make no cache access, so "per access" is not "per load".
- **Atomics, load-exclusives and software prefetches** are not demand accesses at the L1D and are not in `A` (1.1M atomic hits against 365.9M demand hits in gin-2121).
- **AMAT is not pipeline cost.** Misses overlap. A store accesses the cache after it commits, so its wait holds a store-queue entry, not the pipeline; loads and stores are in one data bar.
- **Translation time** (TLB misses, page walks) is before the access and not in it.
- **With a perfect L1** the side has no accesses and shows N/A. With a perfect L2 the parts of the new misses show N/A; the L1 shares and the AMAT total remain.

## Top-down

Shares of the rename slots (`core.topDown.level1.slots` = rename width × cycles), recomputed from gem5's counters so they can be summed over blocks, and checked block by block against gem5's own formulas (`core.topDown.*`). The reasons, the differences from Yasin's paper and the known problems are in [[top-down-accounting]]; only the formulas are repeated here.

`S` = `core.topDown.level1.slots`; `w` = `S` / `core.topDown.cycles`; `rn.` = `core.rename.`; `td.` = `core.topDown.`.

| metric | key | formula |
|---|---|---|
| Retiring | `td_retiring` | `core.commitStats0.numOps` / `S` |
| Bad speculation | `td_badspec` | (`rn.renamedInsts` − `numOps` + `rn.refillBubbles` + `w` × `rn.status::Squashing`) / `S` |
| Front-end bound | `td_frontend` | `rn.fetchBubbles` / `S` |
| Back-end bound | `td_backend` | 1 − retiring − bad speculation − front-end bound |
| Branch mispredicts | `td_bs_branch` | bad speculation × `core.iew.branchMispredicts` / (`branchMispredicts` + `core.iew.memOrderViolationEvents`) |
| Machine clears | `td_bs_clears` | bad speculation − branch mispredicts |
| Fetch latency | `td_fe_latency` | `rn.fetchLatencyBubbles` / `S` |
| Branch resteers (part of fetch latency) | `td_fe_resteer` | `rn.resteerBubbles` / `S` |
| Fetch bandwidth | `td_fe_bandwidth` | front-end bound − fetch latency |
| Serialize | `td_be_serialize` | `w` × `rn.status::SerializeStall` / `S` |
| Memory bound | `td_be_memory` | (back-end bound − serialize) × (`td.loadStallCycles` + `td.storeStallCycles`) / `td.execStallCycles` |
| Core bound | `td_be_core` | back-end bound − serialize − memory bound |
| L1D bound | `td_mem_l1` | memory bound × (`loadStallCycles` − `td.loadStallL1Miss`) / (load + store stall cycles) |
| L2 bound | `td_mem_l2` | memory bound × (`loadStallL1Miss` − `td.loadStallL2Miss`) / (load + store stall cycles) |
| Past L2 | `td_mem_ext` | memory bound × `loadStallL2Miss` / (load + store stall cycles) |
| Store bound | `td_mem_store` | memory bound × `storeStallCycles` / (load + store stall cycles) |

- Runs made before the 2026-11-13 fix report front-end bound too high and back-end bound too low; do not compare shares across that date.
- Memory bound understates what a perfect L1D saves (it predicted +14% for agentic and +17% for SPEC against +43% and +47% measured).
- A split with no events puts its parent's share in its last part.

Evidence for the notes: [[2026-11-13-top-down-microbenchmarks]], [[2026-11-12-spec-400m-sweeps]].

## Scheduling (agentic)

From the guest task trace (`tasktrace.jsonl`), not from gem5 statistics. Agentic runs only have enough switches to read; the values are the same at every edge setting.

| metric | key | unit | source | formula | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Context switches; process, thread, kernel-thread switches | `switches`, `switch_process`, `switch_thread`, `switch_kernel` | per M insts | switch events of the trace, by kind | count / (`insts` / 10⁶) | Kind by thread group: another process, another thread of the same process, or to or from a kernel thread. |
| Switch frequency | `switch_khz` | kHz | switch events; the window's ticks | count / milliseconds of simulated time | Per slice category: switches into it over its own time. Simulated time includes WFI. |
| Time between switches | `switch_gap` | µs | switch events; the window's ticks | simulated time / count | Per slice category: the mean time one of its slices runs. |
| Scheduler instructions | `sched_share` | % insts | trace events at `__schedule` and `__switch_to` | instructions between the two / `insts` | The scheduler's direct cost only: not the switch's tail, nor the trap that led to it. |
| Time share, instruction share (per slice category) | `time_share` | % time, % insts | the scheduling slices | the category's ticks (instructions) / the window's | A slice goes to the category of the task switched in. Idle time is in "Other". |

## Not on the comparison page

`window_view.METRICS` also defines issue rate, BTB misses, FTQ full, decode blocked, all mispredicts, fetch squashing, memory-order violations, rename blocked, registers full, LQ and SQ occupancy and the Garfield shares. They appear in the window view only and are described there by their tooltips.
